## Understand Extraction Types

The extraction type you choose is the single most significant factor determining your pipeline's latency, cost, and complexity. It defines the contract between your source systems and your lakehouse. Selecting the wrong pattern leads to either wasted compute resources or stale data that fails to meet business SLAs. Azure Databricks supports three primary extraction patterns, each with distinct operational characteristics and trade-offs.

### Full Extraction

Full extraction reads the entire dataset from the source system during every ingestion run, completely replacing the destination table.

This approach is the simplest to implement and guarantees consistency, as it eliminates the risk of missing incremental changes. It is ideal when:

-   Source systems lack reliable change tracking mechanisms (e.g., no updated timestamps or CDC).
-   Data volumes are small enough that reprocessing the entire dataset is computationally trivial.
-   Business logic requires periodic full rebuilds to correct drift or apply complex transformations that cannot be applied incrementally.
-   Data relationships are so intricate that incremental logic would be fragile and error-prone.

> [!WARNING]
> **Scalability Ceiling**
> Full extraction has a hard limit. As data volumes grow from gigabytes to terabytes, the cost and duration of each run increase linearly. Reprocessing millions of records when only a few thousand have changed is inefficient and delays downstream analytics. Reserve this pattern for reference data, small dimension tables, or sources where incremental capture is technically impossible.

### Incremental Extraction

Incremental extraction processes only new or changed records since the last successful run. The engine maintains state to track the high-water mark (e.g., last processed timestamp or sequence ID) and queries the source solely for deltas.

This pattern dramatically improves efficiency for large, growing datasets. Instead of scanning millions of rows, you process only the thousands that have changed. However, it introduces complexity:

-   **Reliable Change Indicator:** The source must provide a monotonically increasing column (timestamp, sequence number, or version) that reliably indicates changes.
-   **State Management:** You must persistently track the last processed position to ensure exactly-once or at-least-once semantics across restarts.
-   **Late-Arriving Data:** You need logic to handle records that arrive out of order or after the high-water mark has advanced, which may require late-data handling windows or reprocessing strategies.

> [!NOTE]
> **Change Data Capture (CDC)**
> For databases, CDC is often superior to timestamp-based incremental extraction. CDC captures inserts, updates, and deletes via transaction logs, providing a complete change history without relying on potentially unreliable update timestamps. Unity Catalog’s streaming tables and Delta Live Tables natively support CDC patterns.

### Streaming Extraction

Streaming extraction provides near real-time data capture through continuous processing. Rather than running on a schedule, streaming jobs remain active and process records as they arrive from message buses, change feeds, or file drops.

Consider streaming when:

-   Business processes require data freshness measured in seconds or minutes (e.g., fraud detection, real-time dashboards).
-   Source systems publish events to Kafka, Event Hubs, or database change feeds.
-   You need to trigger immediate actions based on incoming data (e.g., alerts, recommendations).

Streaming delivers the lowest latency but requires always-on infrastructure and careful handling of watermarking, late data, and checkpointing to ensure correctness.

### Choosing the Right Pattern

| Factor | Full Extraction | Incremental Extraction | Streaming Extraction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Latency** | Hours to Days | Minutes to Hours | Seconds to Minutes |
| **Compute Cost** | High (scales with total volume) | Low (scales with change volume) | Moderate (always-on, scales with throughput) |
| **Complexity** | Low | Medium (state + late data) | High (watermarks, checkpoints, ordering) |
| **Source Requirement** | None | Reliable change indicator or CDC | Event stream or change feed |
| **Best For** | Small/reference data, no CDC | Large transactional tables, batch ETL | Real-time analytics, event-driven architectures |

> [!TIP]
> **Hybrid Approaches**
> Many production pipelines combine these patterns. Use streaming for real-time operational data, incremental extraction for large fact tables updated hourly, and full extraction for small dimension tables refreshed nightly. The key is matching the extraction type to the specific latency and volume requirements of each data asset, not applying a one-size-fits-all approach.
